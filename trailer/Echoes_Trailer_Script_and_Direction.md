# ECHOES AMONG THE STARS — Announce Trailer
## Script, shot direction and image reference notes

Target length: **45 seconds** (works as a 30s cut too — trim points marked)
Aspect: 16:9, letterboxed to 2.39:1 in the edit for a cinematic feel
Format: hero shot in 5 chained segments + 10–12 interstitial clips

---

## 1. The core idea

One continuous "hero shot" of the badge, interrupted by jump cuts to the crew. The badge shot is the spine; the crew cuts are the noise. The badge shot resolves at the end — hand, chest, gravity, line — and that resolution is the trailer's payoff.

**The one big decision I'd push you on:** don't show Amelia's face until the very last frame, or not at all. Hands, shoulder, chest, the back of her head, a jaw at the edge of frame. Two reasons:

1. It sidesteps the hardest problem in AI video — face consistency across generations. You get a clean-looking trailer without fighting the tech.
2. It's genuinely better direction. The player *is* Amelia. Withholding her face for 45 seconds and then landing on her portrait as the title card hits is a stronger reveal than showing her at second three.

---

## 2. The badge

Not defined anywhere in your existing docs, so this is a proposal — change freely, but lock it before you generate anything, because it appears in five separate clips and needs to match.

- Brushed titanium oval, roughly 60mm tall, slightly domed
- Deep teal enamel inlay (matches her flight suit)
- Thin brushed-gold rim (matches the gold accents on the suit)
- Embossed motif: a planetary ring crossed by a seven-pointed star
- Micro-etched below: **A. DRAKE** and a service number
- Magnetic backing plate, visible when it tumbles
- **A small indicator LED on the lower edge.** Slow blue pulse when unclipped. Steady green (#5EE898 — her accent colour in the game) the instant it seats against her suit.

That LED is the whole shot. Blue, blue, blue, spinning in the dark for thirty seconds — then green. Do it as a single-frame change with a subtle bloom, no fanfare.

## 4. Shot list

**0:00 – 0:07 · badge alone**

Black frame. The badge drifts into view from lower left, rotating slowly on a lazy off-axis tumble — maybe one full rotation every four seconds. A single hard light source rakes it from the left, catching the gold rim as it turns. Everything beyond the badge falls into deep black. Dust motes drift, unhurried.

Camera: extremely slow push in. Almost imperceptible.
Audio: sub-bass drone. A distant, irregular structural creak. No music yet.

## 5. Audio design

- **0:00–0:07** — sub drone only. Distant creak. Let it feel almost too quiet.
- **0:07–0:18** — metallic ping on each cut. Drone rises slowly.
- **0:18–0:28** — drone becomes rhythmic, a pulse under it. Building.
- **0:28** — **hard cut to near silence.** This is the trick. The montage stops and the audience notices the quiet before they notice anything else.
- **0:32** — the magnetic click, isolated.
- **0:36** — chime, then a heavy mechanical thunk, then room tone.
- **0:39** — a single low string or synth note enters under her line.
- **0:43** — full theme swells on the title card, three seconds, out.

If you're using library music, look for something sparse and low with no percussion until 0:39. Most trailer music is far too busy for this.

## 6. Image reference notes

### The core problem
Your existing portraits are transparent-background PNGs at roughly 600×900, lit flat for compositing over a background layer. Animating those directly will look like a cut-out sliding around. They're the wrong starting point for video.

### The workflow I'd suggest
For each crew member you want in the montage:

1. **Take the existing portrait into an image model as a character reference**, not as the frame to animate.
2. **Generate a new still**: same face, but placed in a real location, lit by that location, at a different framing than the portrait (three-quarter, profile, over-shoulder). This still is what you feed to the video model.
3. **Upscale to at least 1920×1080** before animating.
4. **Animate with a short, small instruction.** One movement per clip. "Slowly turns head to look off camera" beats any longer description. The clips are one second — you don't need much to happen.

### Lock these before generating anything

**Palette:**
- Station interiors: warm amber practicals, deep teal shadows, everything slightly underexposed
- Anything near a viewport: cold blue-white Europa light on one side
- Lab and comms: sickly green-white
- Greenhouse: the only warm, saturated space in the trailer

**Lens language:** shallow depth of field throughout. Slight anamorphic squeeze if the model supports it. Practical lens flares only where there's a light source in frame.

**Grain:** add it in the edit, uniformly, at the end. It hides a multitude of sins and unifies clips from different generations.

**Framing rule:** every crew clip is a medium or medium-close. No wides except A1. Confined space is the point.

## 7. Practical generation plan

**Order of work — do it in this order, it saves regenerations:**

1. Badge reference still (3 angles). Get this perfect. Everything else depends on it.
2. H1. This is the longest single beat and the one most likely to need retries.
3. H2 → H3 → H4 → H5, chaining last frame to first frame each time.
4. Assemble the hero shot and check it reads as continuous *before* you generate a single interstitial.
5. Crew stills (8 images).
6. Crew clips (8 short generations).
7. A1 exterior and A6 door.
8. Record or generate VO.
9. Edit, grade, grain, sound.

**Expect to regenerate H2 and H4 several times.** Hands catching objects, and physics changes, are the two things video models find hardest. Budget for it. If H4 won't come out, the fallback is: cut to black on the chime, hold black for half a second, cut back to the badge already hanging straight. It's arguably a better edit anyway.

**If you want to test cheaply first:** generate H1 and H5 only, cut them together with VO1 and VO6 over black in between. That's an eight-second proof of concept that tells you whether the tone lands before you commit to twenty generations.

## 8. Things I'd flag

- **The 30s vs 60s question.** I've written 45s. If this is for a store page or social, 30s is almost always better — people drop off. The trim points are marked. Save the 60s version for a website hero.
- **Text on screen.** I've kept it to the title card. If you need "Wishlist now" or a demo link, put it after the title card as a separate two-second card rather than crowding the main one.
- **Spoilers.** Nothing here reveals Shaw, Lawson, or Izzy. Block B7 hints at a death without showing it. I'd keep it that way — the trailer sells the premise and the mood, not the plot.
- **The fountain pen.** Her character sheet gives her an antique fountain pen as her signature item, which is a lovely detail. It's not in this trailer. If you'd rather the hero object were the pen instead of the badge, it changes the ending beat — she'd click it open rather than straighten it — and loses the LED moment, but gains something more personal. Worth a thought.
